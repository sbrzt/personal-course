# AI in digital humanities research

A project-based course for learning to work with, talk about and reason about AI as a researcher in the digital humanities, with a focus on metascience, bibliographic data, information science, knowledge engineering and open science.

The lessons are Jupyter notebooks with exercises that check themselves. Every lesson runs in your browser, in Google Colab or on your own computer, and most build towards real work on a real dataset: the bibliographic data of the journal [*Bibliothecae.it*](https://bibliothecae.unibo.it/).

## Start here

1. Read [How this course works](how-to.md): ten minutes on running lessons, exercises and checks, and where your answers go.
2. Open [Phase S · Statistics and probability](lessons/phase-s/phase-s.md) and start with S0.1, *Reading formulas*.

## How the lessons are built

- **Nothing taken for granted.** Every concept starts in plain words and pictures; its formula is read symbol by symbol and worked by hand before any code.
- **Sliders to play with.** Change a number and watch a plot or a statistic respond.
- **Simulate first, formula second.** Where it helps, you generate data whose truth you know and see what each method recovers.
- **Exercises check themselves.** A check prints ✅, ❌ with a hint, or ⬜, and never stops the notebook.
- **Every lesson ends on your data or with a write-up**, so the work feeds your research rather than sitting beside it.
- **AI is the subject, and sometimes the tool.** "Why this matters for AI" notes connect each statistical idea to how AI systems are built, evaluated and used in research.
